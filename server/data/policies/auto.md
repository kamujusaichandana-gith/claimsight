# Harborlight Mutual Roadwise Auto Policy

## Coverage

This fictional policy covers a listed private passenger car for collisions with another vehicle or object, overturn, fire, theft, vandalism, hail, falling branches, and glass breakage. Collision coverage pays up to the lower of the vehicle's agreed value or $48,000 per occurrence. Comprehensive coverage pays up to $48,000 after a covered loss. Emergency roadside assistance reimburses towing up to $175 per event, no more than four events per term. A rental car is covered for up to $45 per day and $1,350 total while a covered repair is underway. The policy also includes up to $30,000 per person and $60,000 per accident for bodily injury liability, plus $25,000 for damage to another person's property. Only drivers and vehicles shown on the declarations page are insured. Optional endorsements can add rental-car liability when traveling outside the home state, but they must appear on the declarations page before the loss. A newly purchased replacement car is covered for 14 days while the owner reports it and adds it to the schedule.

## Exclusions

There is no coverage while the car is being used for paid rideshare or delivery work unless a commercial-use endorsement is listed. Deliberate damage, racing, organized speed events, mechanical breakdown, gradual wear, and loss caused by an unlicensed driver are excluded. Theft of a vehicle left unlocked with its keys inside is not covered when that omission materially enables the theft. Personal belongings inside the car are not covered by this policy. A loss occurring outside the United States or Canada is excluded unless a territory endorsement specifically extends coverage. The policy does not pay for damage caused while the car is being used to commit a felony or while the named insured has transferred ownership without notifying Harborlight Mutual. Normal depreciation is not a separate covered loss, and the policy will not pay a penalty imposed by a rental company or repair shop. A driver who regularly uses the listed car but is not disclosed may affect eligibility for a claim, so household drivers should be reported when they begin using it.

## Deductibles and limits

The collision deductible is $750 per covered event and the comprehensive deductible is $250; glass repair has no deductible, but glass replacement uses the comprehensive deductible. The maximum vehicle payment is $48,000, less the applicable deductible and any salvage value. Rental reimbursement begins after a 24-hour repair delay and ends when the vehicle is repaired or the $1,350 maximum is reached. Roadside towing is limited to $175 per event. Liability limits are $30,000 for bodily injury to one person, $60,000 for all bodily injuries from one accident, and $25,000 for property damage. These limits do not increase because multiple insured drivers or vehicles are involved in the same accident. If the vehicle is financed, payment for a total loss may be issued jointly to the owner and recorded lienholder. The insurer may choose repair, replacement, or cash settlement based on the covered damage and vehicle value; an upgrade requested by the owner is not included in the limit.

## How to file a claim

Report an accident or theft to Harborlight Mutual within 30 calendar days after discovery, and contact police promptly if a vehicle was stolen or another person was injured. Use the policyholder portal or call the fictional claims desk at 1-800-555-0148. Give the claim representative the policy number, vehicle identification number, incident date, location, driver details, and a short description of what happened. Do not authorize non-emergency repairs before an inspection, except where necessary to prevent additional damage. Keep receipts for towing, temporary transportation, and emergency repairs. If another driver is involved, exchange contact and insurance details without admitting fault. A late report may delay payment if Harborlight Mutual cannot reasonably inspect the damage. If the car cannot be driven, move it to a secure location and tell the representative where it is stored. The adjuster can explain whether a preferred repair facility is available, but the policyholder may choose another licensed shop. Send any later police supplement or repair estimate under the same claim number rather than opening a duplicate claim.

## Required documents

Submit the completed incident statement, the driver's license information, vehicle registration, and clear photographs of the vehicle and scene. For a collision involving another driver, include the police report or its report number, witness names, and the other driver's insurance details when available. A theft claim requires a police report and all available vehicle keys; Harborlight Mutual may also request proof of purchase or loan information. Rental reimbursement requires an itemized rental agreement and proof of payment. Repair claims need an itemized estimate and final invoice, and towing claims need a dated receipt showing the pickup and destination. Send requested documents within 20 calendar days of the request, or contact the adjuster before that deadline if a record is not yet available. Include the repair shop's tax identification and contact details when a direct payment is requested. If the car was financed, provide the lender's current payoff contact and account reference. Photos should show the full vehicle, close views of damage, and the odometer when safe to capture.

## Claim review timeline

Harborlight Mutual acknowledges a complete auto claim within two business days. An adjuster normally contacts the policyholder within five business days and schedules an inspection within seven business days when the vehicle is available. Straightforward damage claims are generally decided within 15 business days after the required records and inspection are complete. Theft, disputed-fault, injury, and suspected-fraud claims may take up to 45 calendar days for an initial determination. If more time is needed, the adjuster sends a status notice every 15 days explaining what remains under review. Approved repair payments are issued within five business days after agreement on the estimate and payment details. These are fictional service targets, not a guarantee of coverage or payment. A missed inspection appointment can move the decision date, so notify the adjuster as early as possible if the vehicle or driver will be unavailable. Payment timing also depends on the shop confirming the final scope and the policyholder providing current payment instructions. The written determination explains any deduction or limit applied.
