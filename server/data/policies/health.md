# Harborlight Mutual ClearPath Health Policy

## Coverage

This fictional health plan pays for eligible in-network preventive visits, primary care, specialist consultations, urgent care, inpatient hospital stays, outpatient surgery, and prescription medicines on its four-level formulary. Preventive services listed in the plan schedule are covered at 100% when provided in network. The annual covered-service maximum is $250,000 per member, with no lifetime maximum for essential hospital care. In-network emergency stabilization is covered at the in-network cost share even when the nearest emergency department is outside the network. Prescription coverage includes generic and preferred-brand medicines after the pharmacy deductible. Maternity care is covered after enrollment, including prenatal visits, delivery, and 60 days of post-delivery care. A participating telehealth visit has a $15 copayment and may be used for routine primary care. Members can check the online directory before scheduling to confirm a provider's current network status. Laboratory work ordered during a preventive visit is not automatically preventive if it diagnoses a symptom. Coverage follows the service date and plan schedule, and a referral may be required before a specialist visit is eligible for the in-network cost share.

## Exclusions

The plan does not cover cosmetic procedures without medical necessity, experimental treatments not approved by the plan's review committee, non-prescribed supplements, or care received from a provider who refuses to submit required records. Routine dental work, eyeglasses, and elective hearing aids are not included, though separate riders may be purchased. Services obtained outside the United States are excluded except emergency stabilization up to $10,000. A condition that existed before enrollment is not excluded, but treatment received before the effective date is not payable. The plan excludes charges above the provider's usual and customary rate when a member voluntarily chooses an out-of-network provider, except for a protected emergency. Missed appointment fees and personal comfort items are the member's responsibility. A service must be medically necessary and supported by records from a licensed provider to qualify for review. The plan does not reimburse a member for an amount a provider waives or bills as a courtesy adjustment. Confirm that a referral or advance authorization is in place before a scheduled service when the member card says one is required.

## Deductibles and limits

The in-network individual deductible is $1,250 per calendar year and the family deductible is $2,500. After the deductible, members pay 20% coinsurance for inpatient and outpatient hospital services, with an individual out-of-pocket maximum of $6,500 and a family maximum of $13,000. Primary care visits cost $25 and specialist visits cost $45 before the deductible; preventive visits are $0. The separate prescription deductible is $150 per person, waived for generic preventive medicines. Out-of-network services have a $3,000 individual deductible and 40% coinsurance, and do not count toward the in-network out-of-pocket maximum. The annual covered-service maximum is $250,000 per member; prior approval is required for planned admissions and services listed as high-cost in the schedule. Copayments and coinsurance credited to the calendar year are shown on the member's explanation of benefits. The pharmacy deductible is tracked separately and does not satisfy the medical deductible. A family member who reaches the individual maximum has no further in-network cost share for covered services that year, while other family members continue toward the family maximum.

## How to file a claim

In-network providers normally submit claims directly. If a member pays for an eligible emergency or out-of-network service, file a member reimbursement request within 90 calendar days after the service date. Use the Harborlight Mutual member portal or mail the completed form to the address on the member card. Include the member identification number, provider details, service dates, diagnosis code if supplied, and the amount paid. For emergency care, seek treatment immediately and contact the plan within 48 hours after stabilization when reasonably possible. Ask the provider for an itemized bill rather than a balance-only statement. A claim submitted after 90 days can still be considered when hospitalization or another documented circumstance prevented a timely filing. Check the portal for the assigned claim reference and keep it for follow-up calls. If the provider has not billed the plan, ask whether the office can submit electronically before paying the full balance. Payment of a bill alone does not establish that a service is covered or that the billed amount is the member's final responsibility.

## Required documents

Attach the completed reimbursement form, an itemized provider invoice, proof of payment, and any explanation of benefits from another insurer. Pharmacy requests need the prescription receipt showing medicine name, fill date, quantity, and pharmacy. Emergency claims should include the emergency department discharge summary and, when available, the ambulance statement. Planned hospital care requires the plan's authorization reference; the provider can usually submit this in advance. For a denied claim review, include the denial notice, relevant clinical notes, and a signed member authorization allowing the plan to discuss the case with the provider. Keep copies of every submission and send requested clinical records within 30 calendar days of the request. Do not send original medical records because documents are not returned. Redact unrelated financial account numbers from receipts, but leave the patient's name, date, provider, and service description visible. If another insurer is primary, include its final explanation rather than a preliminary estimate. Members who cannot obtain a clinical note should tell the reviewer which provider has been asked to send it.

## Claim review timeline

Electronic provider claims are acknowledged within three business days. A complete routine claim is generally processed within 20 calendar days; a claim requiring additional clinical review may take up to 45 calendar days. The plan notifies the member within ten business days if records are missing and pauses the review while awaiting them. Urgent-care authorization requests are decided within 72 hours, while non-urgent planned-service authorization requests are reviewed within five business days. An appeal of a denied claim must be filed within 180 calendar days of the notice and is normally decided within 30 calendar days for care not yet received or 60 calendar days for completed care. These fictional timelines do not replace any rights stated in applicable law. Processing time begins after the claim reaches the plan with enough information to identify the member and service. A request for records includes a response date and a secure submission method. Members can request the status by claim reference, and a review decision explains the benefit provision used and any next appeal step.
