# Harborlight Mutual Hearthstone Home Policy

## Coverage

This fictional policy insures the residence and detached structures at the address on the declarations page against sudden direct physical loss from fire, smoke, lightning, windstorm, hail, theft, vandalism, and the weight of ice or snow. The dwelling limit is $425,000 and includes reasonable rebuilding with materials of similar grade. Detached sheds and fences are covered up to $42,500 total. Household belongings are insured up to $212,500, including a special $2,000 limit for jewelry stolen in one occurrence. If a covered loss makes the home uninhabitable, additional living expenses are available for up to 12 months or $85,000, whichever comes first. Sudden accidental pipe leaks are covered when the homeowner takes reasonable steps to protect the property and promptly shuts off the water. A covered loss to built-in appliances is considered part of the dwelling when the appliance is permanently installed. The policy also pays reasonable debris removal within the applicable property limit, and permits emergency board-up work to prevent additional covered damage. It does not insure a second residence unless that address is separately listed.

## Exclusions

Floodwater, storm surge, earth movement, settling, mold caused by long-term moisture, insect or rodent damage, and routine maintenance are not covered. Sewer or drain backup is excluded unless the optional $10,000 endorsement appears on the declarations page. A vacancy lasting more than 60 consecutive days suspends theft and vandalism coverage unless Harborlight Mutual approves an extension in writing. The policy excludes business inventory, motor vehicles, and losses intentionally caused by an insured household member. It does not pay the cost to replace an entire undamaged roof merely to achieve a uniform appearance. Water seepage that develops over weeks or months is not treated as a sudden pipe leak, even if the damage is discovered on one day. Damage caused by a contractor's faulty work is excluded, although resulting sudden damage from a separate covered peril may still be reviewed. The policy does not cover a home used primarily as a short-term rental unless a hosting endorsement is active. Ask the agent to record major renovations before work begins.

## Deductibles and limits

The standard deductible is $1,500 for each covered occurrence. A separate wind and hail deductible of 2% of the dwelling limit, currently $8,500, applies to windstorm or hail losses instead of the standard deductible. The dwelling payment cannot exceed $425,000, and detached structures share a $42,500 limit. Personal property has a $212,500 aggregate limit, subject to lower category caps such as $2,000 for jewelry stolen in one event and $1,500 for cash. Additional living expenses stop at 12 months or $85,000, whichever is reached first. The optional sewer-backup limit does not raise the dwelling limit and has a separate $1,000 deductible. A deductible applies once to each distinct event, not separately to every damaged room or item. The policyholder is responsible for costs above a category cap even when the overall property limit has not been reached. These dollar amounts are the limits for the listed address and do not automatically rise after a renovation.

## How to file a claim

Notify Harborlight Mutual within 14 calendar days after discovering damage, even when the full repair cost is not yet known. For an emergency, protect people first, shut off utilities when safe, and make temporary repairs to prevent further loss. Photograph the damage before cleanup, keep damaged samples when practical, and save every emergency expense receipt. File through the policyholder portal or call 1-800-555-0148 with the policy number, date of loss, address, likely cause, and a safe contact time. Do not discard damaged belongings until the adjuster records them. For theft or malicious damage, make a police report within 48 hours and provide the report number. The claim representative may arrange an inspection before permanent repairs begin. If a roof opening or broken window is exposed, arrange a temporary covering and keep the contractor's invoice. Tell the adjuster whether anyone is still living at the property and where temporary lodging is needed. Keep a dated list of calls and reference numbers so later repair estimates can be attached to the original case.

## Required documents

Provide the signed property-loss statement, photographs or video of each affected room and exterior area, and an itemized list of damaged belongings showing age, purchase price, and estimated replacement cost when known. Include the contractor's detailed repair estimate, emergency mitigation invoices, and proof of payment for temporary lodging if additional living expenses are requested. Water-loss claims should include the plumber's findings about the failed pipe and when the leak likely began. Theft claims require a police report and receipts, appraisals, or other ownership evidence for high-value items. A mortgage statement or deed may be requested to confirm payment interests. Send available records within 30 calendar days; request an extension from the adjuster if contractor reports or official records are delayed. For building materials, include measurements and quantities rather than a single total estimate. The adjuster may ask for maintenance records or prior inspection photos to distinguish sudden damage from wear over time. If a receipt is unavailable, provide a reasonable explanation and another record that supports ownership and value.

## Claim review timeline

The property claims team confirms receipt within two business days and usually schedules an on-site inspection within six business days. The initial coverage review is normally completed within 20 business days after the inspection and required documents arrive. Complex structural damage, a disputed cause, or a late-discovered pipe leak may require up to 50 calendar days for an initial decision. The adjuster provides a written status update every 15 days while a review remains open and identifies any missing evidence. Once a repair scope is agreed, the first approved payment is generally issued within five business days, less the deductible and any applicable limits. Supplemental repair estimates should be submitted before work is concealed, and are usually reviewed within ten business days. Weather emergencies affecting multiple homes can delay inspections, in which case the claims team records the revised appointment date. A second inspection may be needed when hidden structural damage appears during repair. The final payment review checks invoices against the approved scope and may ask the homeowner to explain a material difference.
